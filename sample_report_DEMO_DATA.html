Tax playbook for a Malaysian tax resident

General information, not tax advice. Checked 12 Nov 2026; Budget announcements each October can change these. Confirm with LHDN or a licensed tax adviser before acting.

  1. Capital gains on listed shares are not taxed for individuals. Malaysia's CGT (from 2024) targets companies selling unlisted shares; RPGT covers property. Selling Bursa or foreign listed shares at a profit is generally tax-free for you, as an investor.
  2. Watch the "trader" line. If you trade very frequently (lots of short-term in-and-out trades, using borrowed money, treating it like a job), LHDN can treat gains as business income taxed at your income-tax rate. Use the short-term list sparingly; the long-term list is the tax-friendliest.
  3. Foreign-sourced income (FSI) is exempt for individuals until 31 Dec 2036 when remitted to Malaysia, on condition it was taxed abroad (the 30% US withholding counts). So foreign dividends and gains you bring home are generally not taxed again in Malaysia. Some older articles still say 2026; the gazetted extension and PwC (Jun 2026) say 2036.
  4. US dividends lose 30% at source (Malaysia has no tax treaty with the US covering this), and you can't reclaim it. Ways to cut it:
    • Prefer low/no-dividend growth stocks when holding US names directly (most of the long-term list already is).
    • Hold US index exposure through Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs (e.g. CSPX/VUAA for the S&P 500, CNDX for the Nasdaq-100, VWRA for the world). The fund suffers only 15% on US dividends, and accumulating share classes reinvest them so you get no taxable payout. On a ~1.3% yield that saves ~0.2% a year, compounding.
  5. US estate tax risk. If a non-US person dies holding over US$60,000 of US-situs assets (US company shares and US-domiciled ETFs, even at a Malaysian/Singapore broker), the estate may owe US estate tax of up to 40% on the excess, with only a ~US$13k credit. Irish UCITS ETFs and Bursa shares are not US-situs. If your US holdings pass ~US$60k, shifting the ETF part to UCITS is the easiest fix.
  6. Bursa dividends: single-tier, so tax-free to you, except the 2% dividend tax (from YA2025) on Malaysian-company dividends above RM100,000 a year. ASNB, EPF and unit trust distributions are excluded.
  7. Trading costs as a "tax": Bursa stamp duty is 0.1% of value, capped at RM1,000 per contract note (SC-approved ETFs are exempt until end-2028). Add brokerage and clearing fees, and the FX spread on USD trades; frequent trading costs add up faster than most taxes.
  8. Use the reliefs first, they are a guaranteed return: PRS contributions up to RM3,000 relief (until YA2030); EPF (incl. voluntary/i-Saraan top-ups) up to RM4,000 within the RM7,000 EPF + life insurance bucket; SSPN up to RM8,000 if you save for a child's education (final year YA2027 unless extended). At a 24–25% marginal rate, RM3,000 into PRS saves ~RM720 of tax immediately.
  9. Currency: USD assets carry MYR risk both ways. It is not a tax, but it moves your ringgit returns as much as a tax would. The macro table tracks MYR/USD.

How to read this page

  1. Every stock gets three marks out of 100: Short term (next few weeks to 3 months), Medium term (about a year) and Long term (3–10 years). A mark of 80 means it looks better than 80% of the stocks being watched. It is not an 80% chance of going up.
  2. ▲ Strong = 75 or more, ● Fair = 50–74, ▼ Weak = under 50.
  3. "Why" lists the top reasons for the mark in plain words.
  4. Always check the ⚠ risk tags before buying, especially red "short-seller report" tags.
  5. Click any row to see all the numbers, tax notes for a Malaysian investor, and links to Yahoo, TradingView, Finviz and Bursa.
  6. Use the search box and US / Malaysia buttons at the top to narrow the list, and tick Hide risky to show only stocks without warning flags.
  7. NEW = found automatically this run. auto = found automatically earlier. Others are from your own watchlist.

What goes into each mark: Short = recent price rise, trend, healthy momentum and trading volume. Medium = 12-month momentum, profit and sales growth, analyst targets. Long = fast sales growth, high margins, real cash generation, sensible price for its growth, low debt, room to grow, and low dividend tax for a Malaysian investor.

This is a research tool, not financial advice. No tool can reliably tell which stock will double; most high scorers won't, and some low scorers will. Data comes from Yahoo Finance (free, sometimes late or wrong, especially for smaller Bursa stocks) and FRED.
